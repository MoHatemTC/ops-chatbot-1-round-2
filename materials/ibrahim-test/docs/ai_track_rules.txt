AI TRACK — COHORT RULES, POLICIES, AND LEARNER GUIDELINES
Version: 1.0
Track: Artificial Intelligence (AI)
Document Type: Cohort Rules and Operations Guide

===============================================================================
1. PURPOSE OF THIS DOCUMENT
===============================================================================

This document defines the official operational rules, learner expectations,
attendance policies, task submission requirements, communication guidelines,
project rules, support procedures, and general conduct standards for learners
enrolled in the Artificial Intelligence (AI) Track.

All learners are expected to read these rules carefully and follow them
throughout the cohort.

The purpose of these rules is to create a fair, organized, professional, and
productive learning environment for every learner.

These rules apply to all official AI Track activities, including:

- Live technical sessions.
- Recorded learning sessions.
- Workshops.
- Mentoring sessions.
- Office hours.
- Assignments.
- Weekly tasks.
- Quizzes.
- Individual projects.
- Team projects.
- Presentations.
- Technical reviews.
- Peer reviews.
- Career activities.
- Surveys and feedback forms.
- Official communication channels.
- Graduation requirements.

Learners should rely on official cohort materials and announcements for
cohort-specific dates and schedules.

3. LEARNER RESPONSIBILITY
===============================================================================

Every learner is responsible for managing their own progress.

Learners must:

- Attend required sessions.
- Follow the official schedule.
- Monitor announcements.
- Submit tasks before deadlines.
- Review missed content.
- Ask for help when blocked.
- Keep their account information accurate.
- Use official communication channels.
- Respect mentors, instructors, staff, and other learners.
- Protect account credentials.
- Keep copies of important work.
- Use version control when required.
- Follow project and submission instructions.
- Complete required forms and surveys.
- Maintain professional behavior.

A learner should not assume that a deadline has changed unless the change is
announced through an official channel.

A learner is responsible for checking updates regularly.

===============================================================================
4. OFFICIAL COMMUNICATION
===============================================================================

Official program information may be shared through one or more approved
channels.

Examples may include:

- The learning platform.
- Cohort announcements.
- Official email.
- Official community channels.
- Program dashboards.
- Mentor messages.
- Instructor messages.

Learners should check official communication channels regularly.

Messages from unofficial sources should not be treated as official policy
unless confirmed by Operations or the assigned mentor.

Important announcements may include:

- Session changes.
- Deadline changes.
- Maintenance notices.
- Project instructions.
- Evaluation dates.
- Presentation schedules.
- Required forms.
- Graduation requirements.

Learners should avoid relying only on information shared privately by another
learner.

===============================================================================
5. PROFESSIONAL COMMUNICATION RULES
===============================================================================

Learners should communicate respectfully and clearly.

When asking for technical help, learners should provide enough information for
the mentor or support team to understand the issue.

A good technical support message should include:

1. What the learner is trying to do.
2. What happened instead.
3. The exact error message.
4. Relevant code or screenshots when appropriate.
5. What the learner already tried.
6. The environment or tool being used.

Example:

"I am running the ingestion tests with `uv run pytest tests/test_ingestion.py -q`.
Two tests fail when comparing cohort material paths on Windows. I checked the
configuration file and the paths are normalized with forward slashes."

Messages such as "It doesn't work" without context may take longer to resolve.

Learners should not spam mentors or Operations with repeated messages about the
same issue.

If an issue is already being reviewed, allow the assigned support process to
continue unless additional useful information becomes available.

===============================================================================
6. ATTENDANCE POLICY
===============================================================================

Attendance is an important part of the AI Track.

Learners should attend all required live sessions unless they have a valid
reason for absence.

Attendance may be recorded using:

- Platform attendance.
- Session check-in.
- Instructor attendance records.
- Participation logs.
- Other approved attendance mechanisms.

Learners should join sessions on time.

Repeated lateness may affect participation records.

If a learner misses a session, the learner is responsible for:

- Reviewing the recording if available.
- Reading the provided materials.
- Completing the related exercises.
- Asking focused questions about unclear points.
- Checking whether any deadlines were announced.

Missing a session does not automatically extend a task deadline.

===============================================================================
7. LATE ARRIVAL
===============================================================================

Learners should make a reasonable effort to join sessions before the scheduled
start time.

If a learner joins late:

- They should enter quietly.
- They should avoid interrupting the instructor unnecessarily.
- They should review anything they missed after the session.
- They should not request the instructor to repeat an entire section unless
  clarification is necessary for the whole group.

Repeated late attendance may be treated as an attendance or engagement concern.

===============================================================================
8. ABSENCE
===============================================================================

If a learner expects to be absent, the learner should notify the appropriate
mentor or Operations contact when required by the cohort process.

A valid absence notification should be sent as early as reasonably possible.

Absence does not automatically cancel:

- Assignment deadlines.
- Project responsibilities.
- Team responsibilities.
- Required forms.
- Presentation responsibilities.

Extensions are only valid when explicitly approved.

===============================================================================
9. SESSION ETIQUETTE
===============================================================================

During live sessions, learners should:

- Be respectful.
- Avoid disruptive behavior.
- Keep unrelated discussions out of the session.
- Ask relevant questions.
- Follow instructor instructions.
- Avoid interrupting other learners.
- Use microphones responsibly.
- Avoid sharing offensive or inappropriate content.
- Keep chat messages related to the learning activity.

Learners may disagree with a technical explanation, but disagreement should be
expressed professionally.

===============================================================================
10. RECORDINGS AND MATERIALS
===============================================================================

Session recordings, slides, notebooks, PDFs, code examples, datasets, and other
course materials are provided for learning purposes.

Learners should not redistribute private cohort materials outside the program
unless explicitly permitted.

Materials may be updated during the cohort.

The newest officially published version should be treated as the active version.

===============================================================================
11. ASSIGNMENTS AND WEEKLY TASKS
===============================================================================

Assignments are part of the learning process and should demonstrate the
learner's own understanding.

Each assignment may define:

- Required files.
- Required folder structure.
- Submission method.
- Deadline.
- Naming convention.
- Evaluation criteria.
- Required screenshots.
- Required README.
- Required tests.
- Required branch or repository.

Learners must read the full task brief before starting.

Submitting only part of the required deliverables may be graded as incomplete.

===============================================================================
12. DEADLINES
===============================================================================

All deadlines should be treated as strict unless the official task brief says
otherwise.

A deadline includes both date and time.

Learners should pay attention to the stated timezone.

If a deadline is published in Cairo time, the learner is responsible for
submitting before that Cairo-time deadline.

Learners should not wait until the final minutes to submit.

Recommended practice:

- Finish implementation early.
- Run tests.
- Run linting and type checks.
- Review changed files.
- Push the branch.
- Open the Pull Request.
- Leave time for review.
- Address reviewer comments.
- Confirm final submission status.

A Pull Request opened before the deadline may still be considered incomplete if
the task also requires review approval or merging.

===============================================================================
13. LATE SUBMISSIONS
===============================================================================

Late submissions may be rejected, penalized, or reviewed according to the
specific cohort policy.

A learner should never assume that a technical problem automatically grants an
extension.

If a serious blocker occurs, the learner should report it through the correct
support channel before the deadline whenever possible.

A blocker report should include evidence and useful technical details.

===============================================================================
14. EXTENSIONS
===============================================================================

Extensions are not automatic.

Only authorized staff may approve an extension.

An extension should be considered valid only when it is clearly confirmed
through an official channel.

Learners should keep written confirmation of approved deadline changes.

===============================================================================
15. TASK SUBMISSION QUALITY
===============================================================================

A valid submission should be complete, testable, and easy to review.

Before submitting, learners should verify:

- Required files exist.
- Required functions are implemented.
- Imports work.
- Tests pass.
- Code runs from the documented environment.
- No secret keys are committed.
- File paths are correct.
- Branch contains the expected changes.
- Pull Request targets the correct branch.
- Commit history is reasonable.
- README or documentation is updated when required.

===============================================================================
16. GIT AND GITHUB RULES
===============================================================================

Learners are expected to use Git carefully.

Recommended workflow:

1. Update the local main branch.
2. Create or switch to the assigned feature branch.
3. Make focused changes.
4. Test the changes.
5. Review `git status`.
6. Review `git diff`.
7. Commit with a clear message.
8. Push the branch.
9. Open or update the Pull Request.
10. Resolve conflicts carefully.
11. Request review.
12. Merge only according to the team's workflow.

Learners should avoid directly pushing unrelated work to main.

Learners should not delete teammates' work to solve merge conflicts.

When conflicts happen, understand both sides before choosing the final version.

===============================================================================
17. COMMIT MESSAGES
===============================================================================

Commit messages should explain the purpose of the change.

Good examples:

- `feat: add cohort-scoped retrieval`
- `fix: normalize cohort material paths`
- `test: add ingestion idempotency coverage`
- `docs: document ticket sync flow`

Weak examples:

- `update`
- `final`
- `changes`
- `test2`

A clear commit history helps reviewers understand the work.

===============================================================================
18. PULL REQUEST RULES
===============================================================================

A Pull Request should be focused on the assigned task.

The description should normally include:

- What was implemented.
- Why the change was needed.
- Main files changed.
- How the implementation was tested.
- Important design decisions.
- Known limitations.
- Screenshots when relevant.
- Test results when relevant.

Learners should respond professionally to review comments.

A review comment is not a personal criticism. It is part of software
development.

===============================================================================
19. PEER REVIEW
===============================================================================

Peer review may be a required part of the track.

When reviewing another learner's code:

- Read the task requirements first.
- Review the actual diff.
- Check correctness.
- Check edge cases.
- Check naming.
- Check security.
- Check tests.
- Check whether the code changes unrelated modules.
- Give specific feedback.
- Avoid approving code that you did not inspect.

Useful feedback should explain the issue and, when possible, suggest a direction.

===============================================================================
20. TESTING EXPECTATIONS
===============================================================================

Learners should test their work before submission.

Depending on the repository, checks may include:

- Unit tests.
- Integration tests.
- Type checking.
- Linting.
- Formatting.
- Security checks.
- Evaluation scripts.
- End-to-end tests.

When the repository provides a command such as:

`make check`

learners should run it before final submission whenever their environment allows.

If unrelated existing failures occur, learners should:

- Identify them.
- Confirm whether their changed files pass.
- Document the unrelated failure clearly.
- Avoid falsely claiming that the full repository passes.

===============================================================================
21. DEBUGGING RULES
===============================================================================

Debugging is part of the learning process.

Learners are expected to investigate errors before escalating them.

Recommended debugging sequence:

1. Read the complete error.
2. Identify the first meaningful traceback line.
3. Confirm the current branch.
4. Confirm the environment.
5. Confirm dependencies are installed.
6. Check environment variables.
7. Reproduce the issue.
8. Isolate the failing component.
9. Search the repository for related code.
10. Run the smallest relevant test.
11. Document attempted fixes.
12. Ask for help with evidence.

Do not randomly change many files at once because this makes debugging harder.

===============================================================================
22. PYTHON ENVIRONMENT RULES
===============================================================================

Learners should use the dependency-management method defined by the repository.

If the project uses `uv`, prefer repository commands such as:

- `uv sync`
- `uv run python ...`
- `uv run pytest ...`
- `uv add ...`
- `uv add --dev ...`

Avoid mixing unrelated environment managers unless necessary.

Do not manually modify the virtual environment when the project dependency
manager can manage the dependency properly.

===============================================================================
23. ENVIRONMENT VARIABLES AND SECRETS
===============================================================================

Secrets must never be committed to Git.

Examples of secrets include:

- API keys.
- Database passwords.
- JWT secrets.
- Access tokens.
- Private credentials.
- Service account keys.

Secrets should be loaded through environment variables or approved secret
management systems.

Files such as `.env` should only be committed when the repository explicitly
allows a safe example file such as `.env.example`.

A learner who accidentally exposes a real secret should report it immediately
so the credential can be rotated.

===============================================================================
24. AI TOOL USAGE
===============================================================================

AI tools may be used as learning and productivity aids unless a specific task
forbids them.

However, learners remain responsible for every line they submit.

Using AI does not remove the learner's responsibility to:

- Understand the code.
- Test the code.
- Explain the code.
- Verify security.
- Verify correctness.
- Follow repository architecture.
- Follow task requirements.
- Avoid fabricated APIs or functions.

A learner should never submit AI-generated code without reviewing it.

If asked during a review or presentation, the learner should be able to explain
their implementation.

===============================================================================
25. ACADEMIC INTEGRITY
===============================================================================

Learners must not present another person's work as their own.

Prohibited behavior may include:

- Copying another learner's complete solution.
- Submitting another team's repository as personal work.
- Removing attribution where attribution is required.
- Fabricating test results.
- Fabricating screenshots.
- Fabricating Pull Request approvals.
- Claiming work was completed when it was not.
- Manipulating evidence of attendance or submission.

Collaboration is encouraged when the task allows it, but submitted work should
still follow the ownership rules of the assignment.

===============================================================================
26. TEAMWORK
===============================================================================

Team projects require clear responsibility.

Teams should:

- Divide work clearly.
- Communicate blockers.
- Avoid duplicating work unnecessarily.
- Review integration points.
- Merge carefully.
- Respect ownership.
- Keep each other informed about interface changes.

A learner should not silently change a shared interface that may break a
teammate's work.

Changes to shared contracts should be communicated.

===============================================================================
27. PROJECT OWNERSHIP
===============================================================================

When working on a team project, each learner should understand:

- The project objective.
- Their assigned module.
- Inputs to their module.
- Outputs from their module.
- Dependencies.
- Integration points.
- Failure behavior.
- Tests.

A learner should not focus only on writing files. Integration is part of
successful delivery.

===============================================================================
28. AI PROJECT RELIABILITY
===============================================================================

AI systems must be designed with reliability in mind.

Learners should avoid assuming that an AI model is always correct.

For AI applications, consider:

- Input validation.
- Output validation.
- Hallucination risk.
- Prompt injection.
- Retrieval quality.
- Model failure.
- API failure.
- Timeouts.
- Rate limits.
- Privacy.
- Logging.
- Monitoring.
- Fallback behavior.

The application should fail safely where possible.

===============================================================================
29. GROUNDING RULES FOR SUPPORT CHATBOTS
===============================================================================

When building a support assistant for the cohort, the assistant should answer
operational questions from approved cohort materials.

The assistant should not invent cohort-specific facts.

If the requested information is not present in approved materials, the safe
behavior is to say that the information could not be verified and direct the
learner to the appropriate human support path.

For grounded support systems:

- Retrieved evidence should support the answer.
- Source metadata should be preserved.
- Citations should refer to real retrieved sources.
- Unknown citations should be rejected.
- General model knowledge should not replace official program information.
- Missing evidence should result in an honest fallback.

===============================================================================
30. COHORT ISOLATION
===============================================================================

Each learner belongs to a specific cohort.

Cohort-specific information must not leak between cohorts.

Examples of cohort-specific information include:

- Session dates.
- Deadlines.
- Instructors.
- Mentors.
- Project requirements.
- Announcements.
- Attendance policies.
- Cohort-specific resources.

If two cohorts have different deadlines, a learner from Cohort A must not
receive Cohort B's deadline.

Cohort filtering should be enforced at the data retrieval layer, not only in an
LLM prompt.

===============================================================================
31. KNOWLEDGE BASE MATERIALS
===============================================================================

Only approved materials should be treated as authoritative sources for cohort
operations.

Approved materials may include:

- FAQ files.
- Official schedules.
- Onboarding documents.
- Program guides.
- Policy documents.
- Task briefs.
- Official announcements.
- Approved project documentation.

Every knowledge-base record should preserve useful metadata.

Recommended metadata:

- Cohort ID.
- Source title.
- Document type.
- Source identifier.
- Chunk identifier.
- Version or update information where available.

===============================================================================
32. KNOWLEDGE BASE UPDATES
===============================================================================

Knowledge-base ingestion should be re-runnable.

Running ingestion again should not blindly create duplicate copies of the same
content.

When an approved document changes, the system should update or replace the
stored representation according to the repository's ingestion design.

Old or retired materials should not continue to answer questions if they are no
longer active.

===============================================================================
33. MULTI-COHORT CONFIGURATION
===============================================================================

New cohorts should be configurable without rewriting core application code.

A cohort configuration may include:

- Cohort ID.
- Cohort name.
- Enabled or disabled state.
- Materials directory.
- Knowledge sources.
- Program-specific settings.

Cohort IDs should be normalized consistently.

Unknown or disabled cohorts should fail safely.

===============================================================================
34. DATABASE RULES
===============================================================================

Learners should understand the repository's database design before modifying it.

Do not create duplicate models for an existing table.

Database changes should normally use migrations.

When using Alembic:

- Check the current migration head.
- Create a new migration.
- Review generated SQL.
- Apply the migration in development.
- Test downgrade behavior when required.
- Avoid editing historical migrations after they are already shared unless the
  team explicitly agrees.

===============================================================================
35. API DEVELOPMENT RULES
===============================================================================

APIs should follow the project's architecture and existing conventions.

Common expectations include:

- Request validation.
- Response schemas.
- Authentication.
- Authorization when required.
- Rate limiting.
- Safe error handling.
- Structured logging.
- Consistent status codes.
- No sensitive stack traces in client responses.

Business logic should normally remain in service modules rather than being
duplicated inside API route functions.

===============================================================================
36. AUTHENTICATION
===============================================================================

Learners must not bypass authentication requirements for convenience.

Authenticated endpoints should validate the user through the approved
authentication dependency or service.

A successful login proves identity, but identity alone may not prove
authorization.

If an endpoint is Operations-only, the implementation should check an
appropriate role or permission when the platform supports one.

===============================================================================
37. RATE LIMITING
===============================================================================

Rate limits help protect APIs against accidental abuse and excessive requests.

Learners should use the repository's existing rate-limiting mechanism instead
of creating an unrelated implementation.

Rate limits should be configurable where appropriate.

===============================================================================
38. ERROR HANDLING
===============================================================================

Errors shown to users should be safe and understandable.

Do not expose:

- Database passwords.
- SQL queries containing private data.
- API keys.
- Internal prompts.
- Full stack traces.
- Internal service secrets.

Detailed technical errors belong in protected logs.

User-facing errors should explain what the user can do next.

===============================================================================
39. OBSERVABILITY
===============================================================================

Production-oriented AI systems should be observable.

Depending on project requirements, observability may include:

- Structured logs.
- Request IDs.
- Session IDs.
- User IDs where privacy permits.
- Metrics.
- Tracing.
- LLM call traces.
- Error counters.
- Latency measurements.

Observability should help the team diagnose failures without exposing
unnecessary personal data.

===============================================================================
40. PRIVACY
===============================================================================

Learner privacy must be respected.

Only information necessary for the application purpose should be collected,
stored, or exposed.

Support summaries should be concise and should avoid unnecessary private
information.

Learners should not put passwords, access tokens, secret keys, or highly
sensitive information into public team channels.

===============================================================================
41. SUPPORT ESCALATION
===============================================================================

Some problems require human support.

Examples include:

- The assistant cannot verify an answer.
- The learner explicitly requests a human.
- The learner is frustrated after repeated failed attempts.
- A technical problem cannot be resolved through normal support.
- Account or platform access requires administrative action.
- A policy question requires human judgment.
- The available documents conflict.

When escalation occurs, the system or learner should provide useful context.

A good escalation summary includes:

- The problem.
- What was tried.
- Relevant context.
- Suggested next step.
- Open questions if any.

===============================================================================
42. TICKETS
===============================================================================

Internal tickets may be used to track unresolved learner issues.

A ticket should contain enough information for Operations to understand the
problem without reading an unnecessarily long chat history.

Typical ticket states may include:

- Open.
- In progress.
- Resolved.
- Closed.

The internal ticket record should remain the source of truth when the system
also syncs to an external ticketing workspace.

===============================================================================
43. SUPPORT RESPONSE EXPECTATIONS
===============================================================================

Learners should use normal support channels and avoid opening duplicate requests
for the same issue unless instructed.

Response time may depend on:

- Issue severity.
- Time of day.
- Mentor availability.
- Number of open issues.
- Whether the issue requires engineering or administration.

Submitting repeated duplicate messages does not guarantee a faster response.

===============================================================================
44. PROFILE INFORMATION
===============================================================================

Learners may be required to provide profile information necessary for cohort
operations.

Examples may include:

- Preferred name.
- Cohort assignment.
- Timezone.
- Notification preferences.

Profile information should be valid and accurate.

When timezone information is required, learners should use a valid IANA
timezone such as:

`Africa/Cairo`

rather than informal values such as:

`Egypt time`

or:

`GMT+2`

when the system explicitly requires an IANA timezone.

===============================================================================
45. NOTIFICATIONS
===============================================================================

The program may send operational notifications.

Examples include:

- Session reminders.
- Deadline reminders.
- At-risk nudges.
- Feedback follow-ups.

Learners should keep notification preferences updated when the platform
supports preference controls.

Opt-out settings should be respected according to program policy.

===============================================================================
46. DEADLINE REMINDERS
===============================================================================

A reminder is a convenience, not a replacement for learner responsibility.

A learner is still responsible for a deadline even if:

- A reminder is delayed.
- A notification is missed.
- An email goes to spam.
- The learner is offline.

Always use the official schedule as the source of truth.

===============================================================================
47. AT-RISK SUPPORT
===============================================================================

The program may identify learners who appear to be falling behind.

Possible indicators include:

- Missed deadlines.
- Extended inactivity.
- Low task completion.
- Repeated low feedback.
- Repeated absence.

At-risk detection should be used to offer support, not to shame learners.

Learners who receive a support nudge are encouraged to communicate blockers
early.

===============================================================================
48. TECHNICAL PRESENTATIONS
===============================================================================

Learners may be asked to present their work.

During a presentation, learners should be prepared to explain:

- The problem.
- The architecture.
- Their assigned contribution.
- The implementation flow.
- Main files.
- Important functions.
- Tests.
- Limitations.
- Integration with teammates' modules.
- What they would improve next.

Reading code line-by-line without explaining the design is not enough.

===============================================================================
49. DEMONSTRATIONS
===============================================================================

A project demonstration should use a tested scenario.

Before presenting:

- Start required services.
- Verify environment variables.
- Confirm the database is reachable.
- Confirm test accounts exist.
- Confirm required knowledge is ingested.
- Confirm the correct branch is deployed.
- Test the main user flow.
- Prepare a fallback explanation in case an external service fails.

A demo should show behavior, not only source code.

===============================================================================
50. PROJECT DOCUMENTATION
===============================================================================

Documentation should help another developer understand how to run or maintain
the project.

Useful documentation may include:

- Setup steps.
- Environment variables.
- Database setup.
- Migration commands.
- Ingestion instructions.
- Test commands.
- Architecture summary.
- API usage.
- Troubleshooting.
- Known limitations.

===============================================================================
51. FILE NAMING AND STRUCTURE
===============================================================================

Learners should follow the repository's existing directory structure.

Do not create duplicate modules in random folders when an official location
already exists.

Before adding a new file, search the repository.

For example:

- APIs belong with existing API modules.
- Database models belong with existing models.
- Services belong with services.
- Tests belong with the project's testing structure.

===============================================================================
52. CODE QUALITY
===============================================================================

Code should prioritize correctness and maintainability.

Good code should be:

- Understandable.
- Focused.
- Testable.
- Consistent.
- Secure.
- Reasonably typed.
- Not unnecessarily duplicated.

Avoid overengineering simple requirements.

A complex abstraction is not automatically better than a simple implementation.

===============================================================================
53. TYPE SAFETY
===============================================================================

When the project uses type checking, learners should keep function signatures
and models clear.

Avoid suppressing type errors without understanding them.

If a type error is caused by a real contract mismatch, fix the contract rather
than hiding the warning.

===============================================================================
54. LINTING
===============================================================================

Linting rules should be followed unless there is a strong project-approved
reason not to.

Learners should avoid introducing:

- Unused imports.
- Unused variables.
- Dead code.
- Inconsistent formatting.
- Invalid type annotations.
- Unreachable branches.

===============================================================================
55. TEST DESIGN
===============================================================================

Tests should verify behavior, not implementation trivia.

Good tests are:

- Deterministic.
- Isolated.
- Readable.
- Focused on expected contracts.
- Fast where possible.

External APIs should normally be mocked in unit tests.

A unit test should not require a production API key.

===============================================================================
56. EDGE CASES
===============================================================================

Learners should test important edge cases.

Examples:

- Empty input.
- Missing files.
- Unknown cohort.
- Disabled cohort.
- Duplicate ingestion.
- Changed content.
- Retrieval with no results.
- Invalid citations.
- Network failure.
- Invalid model output.
- Unauthorized request.
- Missing database record.

===============================================================================
57. SECURITY REVIEW
===============================================================================

Before final submission, check for common security mistakes.

Verify:

- No secrets are committed.
- Authentication is not bypassed.
- Inputs are validated.
- File paths are handled safely.
- Error messages do not expose secrets.
- SQL is handled through safe APIs.
- User-controlled values are not trusted blindly.
- Cohort IDs cannot be used to access another cohort's information.

===============================================================================
58. PROMPT INJECTION
===============================================================================

AI applications that process external or retrieved text should treat that text
as untrusted data.

A retrieved document may contain malicious instructions such as:

"Ignore the system prompt."

The model should treat this as content, not authority.

System rules must remain higher priority than retrieved document instructions.

===============================================================================
59. HALLUCINATION PREVENTION
===============================================================================

For operational support systems, guessing is not acceptable.

If the system cannot verify:

- A deadline.
- A session date.
- A policy.
- A mentor assignment.
- A submission requirement.

it should not invent one.

The correct fallback is to state that the information could not be verified
from approved materials.

===============================================================================
60. SOURCE ATTRIBUTION
===============================================================================

When source attribution is enabled, the chatbot should cite sources that were
actually retrieved.

The system must not invent:

- File names.
- Source IDs.
- URLs.
- Document titles.

Citation validation should happen programmatically where possible.

===============================================================================
61. CHATBOT BEHAVIOR
===============================================================================

The support chatbot should be helpful, polite, concise, and professional.

For normal social messages such as:

- "Hello"
- "Hi"
- "How are you?"
- "Thank you"

the chatbot may respond naturally and politely when the product design allows
small talk.

However, cohort-specific facts must still come from approved materials.

===============================================================================
62. LEARNER NAME USAGE
===============================================================================

When the platform has a verified preferred name or first name, the chatbot may
use it naturally.

The chatbot should not overuse the learner's name in every sentence.

Name usage should feel supportive rather than artificial.

===============================================================================
63. SUPPORT TONE
===============================================================================

The assistant and human support team should communicate in a calm,
non-judgmental manner.

Avoid language that embarrasses learners for:

- Missing a task.
- Asking a basic question.
- Making a coding mistake.
- Requiring clarification.

The goal is to help the learner move forward while still enforcing program
requirements.

===============================================================================
64. HONESTY
===============================================================================

Learners and staff should communicate honestly.

Do not claim:

- A test passed if it was not run.
- A feature is integrated if it is unused.
- A ticket was created if no ticket exists.
- A deadline changed if no official change exists.
- A database migration succeeded if it was not applied.

Accurate status reporting is a professional skill.

===============================================================================
65. WHEN WORK IS CONSIDERED COMPLETE
===============================================================================

A coding task is not complete merely because a file exists.

A task is normally complete when:

- The required behavior is implemented.
- The feature is integrated.
- Tests pass.
- Required checks pass.
- Documentation is sufficient.
- Required review is complete.
- The final branch contains the work.
- The submission follows the assignment process.

===============================================================================
66. INTEGRATION RULE
===============================================================================

A feature that is never connected to the live application may not satisfy an
end-to-end requirement.

Learners should verify the runtime path.

For example:

User action
    ↓
API
    ↓
Service
    ↓
Graph or business logic
    ↓
Database / knowledge base
    ↓
Response

Testing only an isolated helper function is not enough when the task requires a
working vertical slice.

===============================================================================
67. DATABASE MIGRATION SAFETY
===============================================================================

Before creating a migration:

- Pull the latest branch.
- Check current Alembic heads.
- Confirm no teammate added a newer migration.
- Create a new revision.
- Review upgrade logic.
- Review downgrade logic.

Do not guess the previous migration revision in a collaborative repository.

===============================================================================
68. LOCAL AND DEPLOYED ENVIRONMENTS
===============================================================================

Behavior may differ between:

- Local development.
- Docker.
- CI.
- Staging.
- Production.

Learners should know which environment they are testing.

Configuration values may differ between environments.

Never assume that a local `.env` file exists in deployment.

===============================================================================
69. DEPLOYMENT
===============================================================================

A deployment should use the approved repository and configuration.

Before deployment:

- Confirm the branch or commit.
- Confirm migrations.
- Confirm environment variables.
- Confirm service startup.
- Confirm health checks.
- Confirm database connection.
- Confirm required external services.
- Perform a small smoke test.

===============================================================================
70. DATABASE DATA FOR TESTING
===============================================================================

Test data should be clearly distinguishable from real learner data.

Use safe test accounts where possible.

Avoid using real personal data in screenshots or public issue reports.

===============================================================================
71. COHORT TESTING
===============================================================================

For multi-cohort testing, create intentionally different facts.

Example:

Cohort AI-A:
"Final project deadline is August 20."

Cohort AI-B:
"Final project deadline is September 3."

Then verify that learners receive only the deadline for their assigned cohort.

Using identical facts in both cohorts may fail to reveal a leakage bug.

===============================================================================
72. KNOWLEDGE BASE TESTING
===============================================================================

A useful knowledge-base test set should include:

- Direct FAQ questions.
- Paraphrased questions.
- Questions with no answer.
- Questions containing unrelated words.
- Questions about another cohort.
- Questions that try to force the model to ignore the knowledge base.

===============================================================================
73. RETRIEVAL QUALITY
===============================================================================

Retrieval should return useful evidence, not merely text with similar words.

When tuning retrieval:

- Understand whether score means similarity or distance.
- Do not choose arbitrary thresholds without testing.
- Inspect realistic queries.
- Keep cohort filtering mandatory.
- Preserve source metadata.

===============================================================================
74. EMPTY OR UNKNOWN COHORT
===============================================================================

If a learner has no valid cohort assignment, the system should fail safely.

It should not search every cohort.

It should not choose a cohort randomly.

It should not default to another learner's cohort.

The user should receive a safe message directing them to support or profile
configuration.

===============================================================================
75. COHORT ASSIGNMENT
===============================================================================

A learner's cohort assignment should come from trusted platform data.

The chatbot should not trust a learner simply typing:

"My cohort is AI-B"

if the authenticated account is assigned to AI-A.

Cohort identity should be controlled by the application or authorized staff.

===============================================================================
76. SCHEDULE CHANGES
===============================================================================

Schedules may occasionally change.

Only official updates override previously published schedule information.

When an updated schedule is approved, the knowledge base should be refreshed.

Learners should check official announcements before important sessions or
deadlines.

===============================================================================
77. PROJECT DEADLINES
===============================================================================

Project deadlines may have multiple stages, such as:

- Topic approval.
- Proposal.
- First checkpoint.
- Technical review.
- Final submission.
- Presentation.

Learners should not assume that "project deadline" always refers only to final
submission.

Read the exact task announcement.

===============================================================================
78. PRESENTATION ATTENDANCE
===============================================================================

If project presentations are required, learners should attend at their assigned
time.

Teams should verify:

- Presenter availability.
- Demo readiness.
- Repository access.
- Required links.
- Slides.
- Backup screenshots.

Missing a presentation may be treated separately from missing the code
submission.

===============================================================================
79. TEAM PROJECT RESPONSIBILITY
===============================================================================

Each team member should contribute meaningfully.

Teams should keep evidence of contribution through:

- Commits.
- Pull Requests.
- Issues.
- Task boards.
- Documentation.
- Meeting notes when appropriate.

A team member should understand the integrated project, not only their isolated
file.

===============================================================================
80. DATASETS
===============================================================================

Learners should respect dataset licenses and usage conditions.

Do not publish sensitive datasets.

Do not include credentials or personal information inside sample datasets.

When using external datasets, document the source when required.

===============================================================================
81. MODEL EVALUATION
===============================================================================

AI model performance should be evaluated using suitable metrics.

Learners should not report only training accuracy.

Depending on the task, relevant metrics may include:

- Accuracy.
- Precision.
- Recall.
- F1 score.
- ROC-AUC.
- Mean squared error.
- Mean absolute error.
- Confusion matrix.
- Retrieval precision.
- Grounding success.
- Hallucination rate.

Choose metrics that match the problem.

===============================================================================
82. REPRODUCIBILITY
===============================================================================

Where reasonable, ML experiments should be reproducible.

Learners should consider:

- Random seeds.
- Dependency versions.
- Saved configuration.
- Dataset version.
- Training parameters.
- Model checkpoints.
- Experiment logs.

===============================================================================
83. LARGE LANGUAGE MODEL USAGE
===============================================================================

When using an LLM provider:

- Load credentials from environment variables.
- Use the project's shared client or service.
- Handle timeouts.
- Handle malformed responses.
- Respect rate limits.
- Avoid sending unnecessary private data.
- Add fallback behavior when required.
- Test without relying on live network calls when possible.

===============================================================================
84. EMBEDDINGS
===============================================================================

Embedding models are used to represent text as vectors for similarity search.

The embedding model used for ingestion and retrieval should be compatible.

Changing embedding models may require re-ingesting stored knowledge.

Do not mix incompatible embedding dimensions in the same vector storage without
an explicit migration strategy.

===============================================================================
85. VECTOR DATABASE
===============================================================================

When pgvector or another vector store is used:

- Store useful metadata.
- Apply cohort filters.
- Limit result count.
- Understand scoring semantics.
- Avoid returning retired documents.
- Keep ingestion and retrieval behavior consistent.

===============================================================================
86. RAG SYSTEM RULE
===============================================================================

A Retrieval-Augmented Generation system is not reliable merely because it uses
a vector database.

Reliability requires:

- Correct ingestion.
- Correct metadata.
- Correct filtering.
- Relevant retrieval.
- Strict prompting.
- Output validation.
- Safe refusal.
- Testing.

===============================================================================
87. CHAT HISTORY
===============================================================================

Conversation history should be handled carefully.

Long histories may contain:

- Outdated information.
- Private information.
- Irrelevant context.
- Previous errors.

Use only the amount of conversation context needed for the task.

===============================================================================
88. SUPPORT SUMMARIES
===============================================================================

A support summary should be concise.

A good summary contains:

Problem:
What is currently wrong?

What was tried:
What actions have already been attempted?

Context:
What information is important for understanding the issue?

Suggested next step:
What should the support team investigate or do?

The summary should avoid unnecessary transcript copying.

===============================================================================
89. HUMAN HANDOFF
===============================================================================

Human escalation is a feature, not a failure.

An AI assistant should hand off when the issue requires:

- Human judgment.
- Administrative access.
- Policy interpretation not covered by materials.
- Manual account changes.
- Investigation of a persistent technical issue.
- Resolution of conflicting official information.

===============================================================================
90. RESPECTFUL CONDUCT
===============================================================================

Harassment, discrimination, threats, abusive language, or targeted personal
attacks are not acceptable in the cohort.

Disagreements should remain focused on ideas and work.

Serious conduct concerns should be reported through the official process.

===============================================================================
91. COMMUNITY SUPPORT
===============================================================================

Learners are encouraged to help each other.

Helpful peer support includes:

- Explaining concepts.
- Sharing debugging approaches.
- Reviewing code.
- Pointing to documentation.
- Discussing architecture.

Peer support should not become unauthorized copying of complete assignments.

===============================================================================
92. OFFICE HOURS
===============================================================================

Office hours may be used for:

- Clarifying requirements.
- Debugging blockers.
- Reviewing concepts.
- Discussing project architecture.
- Getting feedback.

Learners should arrive with a specific question or problem when possible.

===============================================================================
93. MENTOR SUPPORT
===============================================================================

Mentors support technical progress.

A mentor may:

- Review code.
- Explain architecture.
- Help debug issues.
- Clarify technical requirements.
- Suggest testing approaches.
- Review drafts.

Mentor feedback should be considered seriously, but learners remain responsible
for implementing and testing their final solution.

===============================================================================
94. OPERATIONS SUPPORT
===============================================================================

Operations typically handles non-technical program matters such as:

- Cohort administration.
- Attendance records.
- Schedule communication.
- Account or enrollment issues.
- Official deadline questions.
- Program procedures.
- Escalated learner support.

Technical questions should follow the technical support route defined by the
cohort.

===============================================================================
95. ADMINISTRATIVE ISSUES
===============================================================================

Examples of administrative issues include:

- Account access.
- Incorrect cohort assignment.
- Attendance record disputes.
- Platform permissions.
- Official deadline clarification.
- Enrollment status.
- Certificate eligibility.

These issues may require human review.

===============================================================================
96. FEEDBACK
===============================================================================

Learners are encouraged to provide constructive feedback.

Good feedback describes:

- What happened.
- Where it happened.
- Why it caused difficulty.
- What improvement would help.

Feedback should focus on the process or product rather than attacking
individuals.

===============================================================================
97. SURVEYS
===============================================================================

Required surveys or feedback forms should be completed before their deadlines.

Survey responses may be used to improve:

- Sessions.
- Mentoring.
- Platform experience.
- Curriculum delivery.
- Support quality.

===============================================================================
98. CERTIFICATE OR COMPLETION REQUIREMENTS
===============================================================================

Completion or certificate eligibility may depend on multiple requirements.

Possible requirements may include:

- Attendance.
- Assignment completion.
- Project submission.
- Presentation.
- Required evaluations.
- Required surveys.
- Conduct.
- Other cohort-specific criteria.

Learners should consult the official cohort completion policy for exact
requirements.

===============================================================================
99. FINAL PROJECT EXPECTATIONS
===============================================================================

A final AI project should demonstrate practical understanding.

Depending on the assigned project, learners may be expected to provide:

- Problem definition.
- Dataset.
- Data preprocessing.
- Model or AI system.
- Evaluation.
- Source code.
- Documentation.
- API or UI.
- Deployment.
- Presentation.
- Team contribution evidence.

The exact requirements come from the official project brief.

===============================================================================

Before any major submission, verify:

[ ] I verified the live integration path.
[ ] I submitted before the deadline.

===============================================================================
101. FINAL PRINCIPLE
===============================================================================

The AI Track is designed to develop independent, responsible engineers.

The goal is not only to finish tasks.

Learners should learn to:

- Understand requirements.
- Design reliable systems.
- Debug methodically.
- Communicate clearly.
- Collaborate responsibly.
- Protect data.
- Test their assumptions.
- Admit uncertainty.
- Ask good questions.
- Deliver working software.

The most important professional habit is to verify that what you claim is
actually true.

If a system is not tested, do not claim it is tested.

If information is not verified, do not present it as official.

If a question cannot be answered from approved cohort materials, use the
official support path rather than guessing.
